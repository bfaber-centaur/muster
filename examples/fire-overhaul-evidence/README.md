# Fire overhaul vs. arson evidence: illustrative lab

This lab is an **illustrative** model of a mechanism that fire-investigation
guidance describes. It is **not** a reconstruction of any particular fire. It
is the fire-service counterpart of Muster's toy result, where containment can
destroy the evidence a later response needs.

After a structure fire is knocked down, crews **overhaul**: they open concealed
spaces and pull out smouldering material so that the fire does not
**rekindle**. Done before an investigator has examined the scene, that work can
destroy the fire patterns and the in-place contents used to establish origin and
cause. An incendiary fire may then never be identified, and the person who set
it remains free.

## Sources (all opened and read for this lab)

- **NIJ**, *Fire and Arson Scene Evidence: A Guide for Public Safety Personnel*,
  NCJ 181584 (June 2000), Technical Working Group on Fire/Arson Scene
  Investigation. https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/181584.pdf
  - Defines overhaul (footnote 5) as "The process of opening concealed spaces to find
    pockets of fire and removing smoldering materials."
  - Lists "Overhaul activities that destroy fire patterns" among threats to
    evidence. Under "Protect evidence by", it lists "Limiting excessive fire suppression,
    overhaul, and salvage."
  - On evidence lost through normal work: "This destruction may be exacerbated
    by the normal and necessary duties of fire personnel carrying out rescue,
    suppression, overhaul, and salvage tasks."
  - On fire size: "The larger a fire becomes and the longer it burns, the less
    evidence of causation will remain."
  - On why detection matters: "Fires of incendiary (i.e., deliberate) cause must
    be detected, so that the firesetter can be intercepted before doing more
    harm". On accidental causes, they "need to be identified" so that dangerous
    products or practices can be dealt with. Also: "all fires are considered
    accidental until they can be proven to be intentionally set".
- **USFA**, *Fire investigation: The first responder's role* (web page).
  https://www.usfa.fema.gov/a-z/arson-fire-investigation/fire-investigations-first-responders.html
  The page says: "Do not overhaul prior to the arrival of the fire investigator."
- **National Fire Academy**, *Fire Investigation: First Responders*, Student
  Manual, 1st ed., 7th printing (Jan 2025). https://apps.usfa.fema.gov/ax/sm/sm_0770.pdf
  It says: "Fire investigation occurs between suppression and overhaul", "Excessive overhaul
  will make the scene examination more difficult to conduct", and "Overhaul
  prior to investigation may have destroyed this evidence". The firefighter's
  lane includes "Delay overhaul & overhaul", and rekindle is listed among the
  actions that can affect the investigation. (USFA notes that the manual may
  contain licensed copyright material, so only short quotes are used here.)
- **NFA F0379 syllabus**. https://apps.usfa.fema.gov/ax/syll/syllabus_f0379.pdf
  It says: "By strengthening the partnership between first responders and investigators, the
  chances for successfully solving arson-related crimes will increase."
- **Phoenix Regional SOP M.P. 202.12B, *Overhaul*** (2022).
  https://www.phoenix.gov/firesite/Documents/074729.pdf
  - The goal of overhaul is to "reduce the possibility of a subsequent fire". Overhaul "may
    not eliminate all the risks of rekindle".
  - On evidence: crews "should compare the importance of preserving evidence to the danger of
    immediately removing debris and extinguishing all traces of fire. In some
    cases, it may be necessary to monitor spot fires until investigators arrive
    on the scene."
  - Lists "Coordinate overhaul with fire investigators" among the IC's tasks.
  - On thermal cameras: "a TIC may not be able to detect small hot spots".
  - On insulation: hot spots "can get buried deep inside cellulose insulation".
- **Codding & Bohn**, "Incident Commander's Guide to Preserving Evidence", *Fire
  Engineering* (Aug 2006).
  https://www.fireengineering.com/firefighting/incident-commanderrsquos-guide-to-preserving-evidence
  Advises crews to "perform the minimum amount of overhaul consistent with the objectives of
  detecting fire and preventing rekindle", and to "make only small holes".
- **Soong**, "Rekindle vs. Spoliation", *Fire Engineering* (Apr 2019).
  https://www.fireengineering.com/firefighting/rekindle-vs-spoliation/
  Cites the NFPA 921 (2017) definitions of overhaul and spoliation. Argues that a crew with a
  thermal imaging camera can leave more of the scene intact, and that excessive
  overhaul can make origin and cause impossible to determine.
- **Facey**, "How to Avoid Spoliation During Overhaul", *Firehouse* (May 2016).
  https://www.firehouse.com/community-risk/article/12202457/how-to-avoid-spoliation-during-overhaul
  It says: "only overhaul and disturb the bare minimum without risking a rekindle", "Let them
  gear up before you begin overhaul", and "photographs are better than nothing".
- **Palumbo**, "Fire Investigation Concerns for Firefighters and Fire Officers",
  *Fire Engineering* (Jan 2026). https://www.fireengineering.com/?p=706997
  Reports that NFPA 921 (2024) added a section 6.8, "Impact of Firefighting Actions".
- **Vincent Dunn**, "Fire Rekindle" (newsletter).
  https://www.vincentdunn.com/downloads/training-minutes/fire-rekindle.pdf
  Calls rekindle "a dirty word in the fire service", and notes that sometimes "the second fire is
  much larger than the first".

**NFPA 921 itself was not read.** The full text is paywalled or requires login.
Statements about NFPA 921 here come second-hand from Soong (2019) and Palumbo
(2026). The USFA, NIJ and NFA material above carries the argument without it.

## Corrections to the framing in the assignment

- The NIJ guide's title and number are confirmed: NCJ 181584, June 2000. The NIJ
  guide does say overhaul destroys fire patterns. It frames this as something to
  *limit*, not something to stop: it calls overhaul one of the "normal and necessary duties".
- None of the sources I read says in so many words that "premature overhaul lets a serial
  arsonist set another fire". The link is two separate statements. (a) Overhaul and
  suppression destroy origin and cause evidence (NIJ, NFA, USFA). (b) Incendiary
  fires must be detected so that the firesetter can be intercepted (NIJ). The model
  joins them. That join is reasonable, but it belongs to the model, not to the sources.
- The idea that a rekindle *itself* destroys origin evidence is my inference. It rests on the
  NIJ statement about fire size and duration, and on Dunn's remark that the second fire can be larger.
  The sources do not say it directly. See the sensitivity check below. Several of the search
  findings depend on it.

## What the compression preserves

| # | Event | Grounding |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Someone deliberately sets the fire (origin evidence + visible indicators) | NIJ 3.A list of indicators |
| 2 | First firefighters arrive | USFA: firefighters are the investigators' "eyes and ears" |
| 3 | Main fire knocked down; hidden fire remains in voids and contents | Dunn; Phoenix "Hidden fires" |
| 4 | Crews turn to overhaul (where the overhaul controls attach) | NIJ fn. 5; Phoenix |
| 5 | Hidden fire flares up after crews leave; *assumed* to burn away the origin evidence | Dunn; Phoenix; NIJ (inference) |
| 6 | Investigator examines origin and cause (needs the scene intact) | NFA; USFA |
| 7 | Arson case opened, firesetter stopped (*simplification*) | NIJ "intercepted before doing more harm" |
| 8 | Leftover fire flares up after the scene is released | Phoenix (fire watch, post-incident walk-throughs) |
| 9 | Firesetter sets another fire | follows from 7 |

Choices:

- **V1** Look for signs of arson on arrival (NIJ 3.A; USFA).
- **P1** Fire watch until the investigator arrives (Phoenix). It blocks the
  rekindle while crews would otherwise be gone, and does not cover the period
  after the scene is released.
- **R1** Full overhaul right away. This is routine practice: it clears hidden fire and
  destroys origin evidence (NIJ 3.B).
- **R2** Call a fire investigator. The investigator is called at knockdown and arrives later.
- **R3** Hold overhaul for the investigator (USFA; NFA "Delay overhaul"). The hold is applied
  only if crews reported arson signs (V1) *and* an investigator is coming. After the
  examination, full overhaul is done.
- **R4** Targeted overhaul with a thermal camera (Codding & Bohn; Soong; Facey). It clears
  hidden fire that a camera can find and leaves the evidence.

## What it omits

Firefighter safety and structural collapse, which often decide overhaul on
their own terms. Also omitted: water and hose-stream damage, salvage, scene
security and chain of custody, utility shutoff, photographs as partial
evidence, the legal side (warrants, spoliation liability), staffing cost of a fire
watch, and the fact that an established incendiary cause very often does *not*
lead to an arrest. Everything is boolean. In reality, partial evidence and partial
overhaul are the norm.

## Variants

- `accidental-fire` (alternative-branch): no firesetter and no arson signs. This tests
  whether the tension disappears when there is no arson.
- `investigator-first` (alternative-branch): the investigator examines the scene between
  knockdown and overhaul, which is the sequence the NFA manual teaches.
- `deep-seated-fire` (alternative-branch): part of the hidden fire is buried where a
  thermal camera and small holes miss it (Phoenix on cellulose insulation). This tests the
  "minimum overhaul" middle path.
- `ems-stages` (perturbation): an ambulance stages nearby. Nothing should change.

There is no `failed-attempt` variant. The lab is illustrative, and there is no
recorded attempt that a defense denied.

## Interpretation

1. **Search does find non-monotone cases, and the mechanism is the one the
   guidance warns about.** In the baseline, adding *routine full overhaul (R1)*
   to a Watchline that was already preserving the scene (`[R2, R4]` or `[P1,
   R2, R3]`) is strictly worse: the investigator arrives to a stripped room. This
   finding does not depend on the rekindle inference. It is the direct content of
   NIJ 3.B and the NFA's "Overhaul prior to investigation may have destroyed this
   evidence". This is a documented mechanism, not a surprise produced by the model.
2. **A hold without a fire watch is worse than no hold.** `[V1, R1, R2]` +
   **R3** goes from {undetermined, another fire} to {rekindle, undetermined,
   another fire}. The same happens when **V1** (spotting arson signs) or **R2** is
   the added piece, because each one completes the trigger for the hold. Holding overhaul
   keeps the hidden fire. If nobody watches it, it flares up, and in this model it burns
   the evidence the hold was meant to protect. Phoenix's "monitor spot fires until
   investigators arrive" is exactly the missing piece: `[V1, P1, R1, R2, R3]`
   reaches no adverse outcome.
3. **Does the reversal vanish for an accidental fire? Partly.** The
   serial-arson harm disappears, and so do the hold-related reversals, because with
   no arson signs the hold never triggers. The "+R1 destroys the scene" reversal
   survives with a smaller harm, an undetermined cause. This is consistent with
   NIJ's point that accidental causes also need identifying. The robust table shows
   a real policy gap: "Hold overhaul with fire watch" gets **worse** under the
   accidental variant (O2 ↑). A hold that is triggered by *arson signs* does nothing
   for accidental fires, so routine overhaul destroys evidence of, for example, a
   wiring or product fault.
4. **When the investigator documents first, every reversal disappears**
   (`investigator-first`: 0 worse, 0 tradeoffs). In this model the conflict is
   entirely about *ordering*. That matches the NFA framing that investigation
   happens "between suppression and overhaul".
5. **Minimum overhaul is the cheapest full defense in the baseline (`[R2, R4]`),
   and also the most fragile one.** It fails completely under `deep-seated-fire`
   (O1, O2, O3 ↑), because the camera misses the buried fire, the fire rekindles, and
   the evidence is lost. The full Watchline (`[V1, P1, R1, R2, R3]`) holds under
   every variant except the accidental-fire gap noted above.
6. The perturbation changes nothing, as expected.

### Sensitivity check: rekindle destroys evidence

I re-ran the baseline search on a scratch copy in which the rekindle does
**not** remove `evidence:origin-patterns`. That copy was not saved in this
directory. Result: 2 worse and 4 tradeoffs, instead of 5 worse and 1 tradeoff.
The two "+R1 full overhaul" reversals remain. The three hold-related "worse"
findings (+R3, +R2, +V1) become **tradeoffs**: a rekindle in exchange for an
established cause. So finding 2 depends on the inference that a rekindle consumes
the area of origin. Finding 1 does not.

## Caveats

- This is an illustrative, boolean model, not operational guidance on how to run a
  fireground. Firefighter safety, structural stability and local SOPs come first.
- "Cause established, therefore firesetter stopped" is a large simplification.
- The claim that a rekindle destroys origin evidence is an inference (see the sensitivity check).
- NFPA 921 was not read directly.
- Engine/CLI note: in the `lab` step trace, a respond control that was *waiting*
  (for example `full-overhaul-now` while overhaul is on hold) is still printed with
  a 🔧 marker on that event. The JSON shows `Disposition: waiting, Acted: false`, and
  the outcomes are computed correctly.
